Free CC0 pixel-art sprite VFX generator

CrunchyVFX makes animated 2D effects for games, in your browser: explosions, hit sparks and slashes, spells and heals, coin and gem pickups, fire, smoke, rain and snow. Start from a game moment (a hit, a death, a pickup, a power-up), press Randomize, breed nine children of an effect you like, or answer the Wizard's three questions and pick one of nine finished effects. Then change the particles, their shapes, the layers, the timing, the palette and the era style.

It is made for hobbyist game developers: people with a game to finish who don't have the time to animate every explosion by hand. For Godot 4 an export is a ready scene with its SpriteFrames, which plays, signals the impact frame and frees itself. Phaser 3 and Aseprite get their own metadata, Unity takes the sheet as it is, and for any other engine there is a generic JSON sidecar. Paste a CrunchySFX sound link and it draws an effect to match, then exports the sound beside it.

Every particle is drawn by code, and every effect is a seeded simulation: no generative AI, no samples and no bundled art, and the app never uploads anything you make. An effect can be shared as a link that holds its whole recipe. Everything CrunchyVFX makes for you is CC0: use it in any game, commercial included, with no attribution. A sprite or image you import yourself keeps its own license.

Free on the web: every generator and every export. No account, no catch, CC0: yours for any game, commercial included, no credit needed.

The desktop app ($6, Windows and Linux) adds the workflow around your game:

  • Saves straight into your game's folder (Godot or Unity), and updates in place: change an asset, re-save it, and every scene using it keeps working.
  • “Update available” and Update all when a new version of the app would export something better.
  • A guard: it warns you before deleting a file your game still uses.
  • Works offline, with your designs kept beside your game rather than in a browser.
  • Game moments: an effect, a sound, a hit look on the target, a screen shake and a hit stop, saved as one Godot node. Your code is one line: $Hit.play_at(enemy).
  • Sounds from CrunchySFX saved in the same game, linked into your effects, plus drag-out export.
  • Palettes that follow you, and the game palette.

Questions

Is CrunchyVFX free for commercial use?

Yes. Every effect you make or export with CrunchyVFX is CC0, which puts it in the public domain: use it in commercial games, videos and apps, edit it and ship it, with no attribution and no royalties. The web version is free, with every generator and every export.

Does CrunchyVFX use AI?

No. Every particle shape is drawn by hand-written code, and every effect is a seeded particle simulation with layers, palettes and a pixel-art finishing stage. There is no generative AI in what it makes, and no sampled or bundled art.

Does it work with Godot?

Yes. Choose Godot 4 in the Export dialog and you get the sprite sheet, a SpriteFrames resource (.tres) and a ready scene (.tscn) with a small script: add it to your game and it plays, sends an impact signal on the hit frame, and frees itself when a one-shot ends. The scene is checked by playing it in a real headless Godot.

Does it work with Unity, Phaser or Aseprite?

Yes. The sprite sheet imports into Unity as a normal sheet: set Sprite Mode to Multiple and slice by cell size. Phaser 3 gets an atlas JSON, Aseprite gets its own JSON with the frame timing, and Copy sheet puts the sheet straight on the clipboard.

What sizes and formats can I export?

Frames are 32 to 256 pixels square, or any width and height up to 512, exported at 1x, 2x, 3x, 4x or 8x. You can save a PNG sprite sheet (grid or strip), PNG frames in a zip, an animated PNG or a GIF, plus an emissive mask for your engine's bloom and, when the effect has a matched sound, a WAV beside it.
